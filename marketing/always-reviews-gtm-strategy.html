Who to target

Local service businesses, not SMBs in general

The pattern that makes Always Reviews valuable: high volume of discrete, completed jobs (so there's always a fresh "ask" moment), a Google Business Profile that actually drives new customers, and an owner too busy to chase reviews by hand. Print Express fits this exactly — that's not a coincidence, it's the filter.

Salons & personal care High repeat-customer volume, strong word-of-mouth culture already, low resistance to a WhatsApp-based tool Instagram/Facebook local business pages

First-wave shortlist criteria

For the initial 15–20: Kimberley-area or Jonas's existing network first (shorter sales cycle, easier to visit in person), an active Google Business Profile with at least a handful of reviews already (proves they care about it), and an owner who is reachable directly — not a franchise with a head-office marketing department.

05
The pitch

Lead with the result, not the mechanism

Nobody buys "n8n workflows and Supabase RLS." They buy "more reviews without doing anything." Keep the pitch to what changes for them.

Opening line (in person / phone)

"Quick question — when a job finishes, does anyone follow up to ask the customer for a Google review, or does it just... not happen?"

Almost every owner's honest answer is "not really" or "sometimes, when we remember." That gap is the entire pitch.

The one-sentence pitch

"We text and email your customers automatically after every job asking for a review, follow up a few times if they don't respond, and reply to the good reviews for you — Print Express went from asking nobody to asking every customer, with zero extra work on their end."

Objection: "We already ask sometimes"

"That's the gap, actually — 'sometimes' means most jobs never get asked. This runs on every single completed job automatically, and stops the moment someone leaves a review or asks to be left alone."

Objection: "Is this just spam texting my customers?"

"No — one ask, then up to three gentle follow-ups spaced 5/10/20 days apart, and it stops instantly if they review, opt out, or you mark them do-not-call. You can see and control the full list any time."

Objection: "What happens if someone leaves a bad review?"

"You find out immediately — the moment it lands, not weeks later by chance. Good reviews get a genuine reply automatically. A bad one goes straight to you, so you're the one who gets to respond personally. That's actually what customers want to see on a bad review — a real reply from the owner, not a bot. It shows you care, not the opposite."

07
Proof

Print Express is the whole sales deck

Founder-led outbound at this stage sells on a shown result, not a slide. Before the first outreach call, get one real, screenshot-able number from Print Express's dashboard — reviews requested vs. received, or the conversion rate stat tile already built into dashboard.html.

Bring a phone or laptop to every conversation and show the live dashboard, not a mockup. "This is a real business, running right now" is the entire trust argument for a founder with one pilot client — use it directly instead of dressing it up as a bigger operation than it is.
