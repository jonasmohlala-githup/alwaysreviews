Outreach Scripts

First-touch WhatsApp messages for prospective business owners — not the customer-facing review-request templates (those are review_request_v1–4, already live in the send workflow). Six drafts, one problem-first framing, ready to send. Medium and with proof are the field-tested defaults — reach for short only when even those feel too long for the contact.

01
How to use these

Six drafts, one job each

Two tones (warm & casual, professional & concise) times three message lengths, all leading with the problem rather than the product — the framing the messaging framework validated after reviewing Review Harvest's "No Reviews / No Time / No Visibility" pattern.

  • Default to medium or with-proof — marked with a gold border below. Short reads correctly but gives up too much on segment-specific pain or peer proof; use it only when the contact clearly prefers brevity over context.
  • Pick one tone per prospect based on how they present themselves (a chatty Instagram bio → warm; a plain Google listing with no social presence → professional) — don't mix tones on the same contact.
  • Send the opener only. Never chain multiple templates into one message.
  • Swap every [Name] and [Business] placeholder — a visibly templated first line is worse than no message.
  • These are first-touch only. If they reply, drop the script and talk like the two humans you actually are — lead with the one-sentence pitch and objection answers from the go-to-market doc's Section 05 if needed.
02
Warm & casual

For businesses with an informal, personal brand voice already — a chatty Instagram/Facebook page, a first-name-basis local reputation. Reads like a message from another small business owner, not a vendor.

Opener — shortany segment

Hey [Name]! Random question — when a job wraps up, does anyone actually follow up to ask the customer for a Google review? Or does it just kind of... not happen?

Fallback only — reach for medium or with-proof first. Designed to get a one-word honest reply ("not really") that opens the door itself — matches the in-person opening line in the go-to-market doc, adapted for text.

Opener — mediumauto repair · home servicesDefault

Hey [Name], quick one — I'd guess [Business] gets a steady stream of happy customers who'd leave you a great review if someone just asked at the right moment. Does that actually happen today, or does it fall through the cracks once the job's done?

Names the segment's real pain (job-based businesses lose the "ask moment" the second the job ends) without naming the product yet.

Opener — with proofprint & copy shopsDefault

Hey [Name] — saw [Business] on Google, looks great. Small thing I noticed: not many reviews for how long you've been around. I built something for Print Express (same kind of shop) that asks every customer automatically after a job — went from asking basically nobody to asking everyone, zero extra work on their end. Worth a 2-minute look at their actual dashboard?

The only draft naming Print Express directly — reserve for the segment where "same category, real peer" lands hardest, per the go-to-market doc's target table.

03
Professional & concise

For businesses presenting more formally — a plain Google Business listing, no chatty social presence, a practice or shop that reads buttoned-up. Same problem-first order, tighter and more direct.

Opener — shortany segment

Hi [Name] — quick question about [Business]: is there a process today for asking customers to leave a Google review after a job's done, or does that mostly not happen?

Fallback only — reach for medium or with-proof first. Same diagnostic question as the warm version, phrased for a reader who prefers directness over chattiness.

Opener — mediumsalons & personal careDefault

Hi [Name], I work with local service businesses on Google review visibility. Most owners I talk to want more reviews but nobody has time to chase them down after every appointment. Is that the case at [Business] too?

"Google review visibility" replaces "reputation marketing," which the messaging framework's voice guide flags to avoid.

Opener — with proofindependent clinics & practicesDefault

Hi [Name] — I've built an automated review-request system for a local business (happy to show you the live dashboard, no obligation). It asks every customer after their visit, follows up a few times if needed, and stops the moment they respond or opt out. Open to a short look at how it works for [Business]?

Leads with consent/control language ("stops the moment they opt out") ahead of the pitch — this segment needs that reassurance earlier, per the go-to-market doc's compliance note on clinics.

04
Follow-up if no reply

Same discipline you're selling

The pitch itself promises "one ask, then a few gentle follow-ups, then it stops." Outreach to business owners should follow the identical shape — it's a small, deliberate proof point if it ever comes up in conversation.

Day 4–5

"Hey [Name], no pressure — just bumping this in case it got buried. Happy to send that dashboard link if you're curious, or just say no thanks and I won't follow up again."

Day 12–14

One more, only if the first got no response at all (not even a "not now"): "Last check-in from me on this, [Name] — if it's not useful right now, all good, I'll leave it there."

After that

Stop. No third follow-up. Mark the prospect as cold and revisit only if something changes (a referral, a new location, a seasonal reason to re-open it).

Which draft, by segment
